# Website content reference — How Scan Book Go works

STATUS: **Future marketing content reference only.** Do not implement UI or publish claims until the Gate 2 functional contracts and CP31 acceptance gates are complete.

## One platform, two ways to operate

**Hotel-managed transfers:** The hotel controls its own property and pays Scan Book Go for the property licence.

**Independent transfer operator:** The hotel retains ownership and explicitly grants an independent operator permission to serve that property. The operator pays Scan Book Go for the property licence and manages the authorised transfer service. The operator's commercial agreement with the hotel is separate from the Scan Book Go subscription.

**Illustrative example:** Kos Transfers serves three authorised hotels. With three €179/month property licences, its illustrative monthly Scan Book Go subscription is €537, subject to the applicable commercial terms. This is not a live quote or a promise of activation.

## What a hotel keeps control of
- Hotel ownership and its hotel identity.
- The right to grant and revoke the operator's property and provider permissions.
- The separate commercial relationship with the operator.

## What the operator manages
- Its own organisation and Scan Book Go subscription.
- Licence allocation only to hotels it is explicitly authorised to serve.
- Transfer operations only after all independent access and activation gates pass.

## Critical distinctions
- A hotel grant is **not** a purchased property licence.
- A paid property licence is **not** permission to serve the hotel.
- Provider authorisation is **not** operational activation.
- Revoking authorisation must block operational access immediately; commercial allocation and billing history are managed separately.
- One active property licence allocation per hotel in V1.
- Guest fares and Scan Book Go SaaS subscriptions are separate payment flows.

This reference describes the intended operating relationship, **not a claim that all functionality is already live**.
